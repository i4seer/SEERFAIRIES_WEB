Case overview

VOSTOK, PENNSYLVANIA • THE MON VALLEY

THE BREAD HAS TURNED TO STONE.

THE EMPTY CHAIR PULLS BACK.

THE FAMILY IS PREPARING TO LEAVE.

Twelve-year-old Katya Volkov-Marsh returns to her late grandfather's row house to find a half loaf hard as stone and a chair that will not stay pushed beneath the table. Her mother is sorting the family's history into keep, donate, and trash. Something in the kitchen refuses to be sorted.

Her neighbor Danya has been keeping a record of the household rules Katya missed. A brown cloth recipe book holds another part of the handoff. With Eilidh Mòr's help, Katya begins to understand the presence her grandfather lived alongside—and what his death left unfinished.

But caring for a household cannot mean being trapped in one house forever. As the sale approaches, Katya and her mother must learn what can travel with them, what they owe the place they are leaving, and how to make a home the spirit has never known.

A home can change without leaving its care behind.
