The compact-keeper

The stories crossed
the ocean, too.

Families carried their trades, their customs, and their stories into new homes. Something older came with them. The creatures of European folklore found new rivers, new mines, new barns, and new doors to stand behind.

For a while, people remembered. They left the bowl. They respected the boundary. They knew which sound meant it was time to turn back.

Then the factories closed, the mines were sealed, and the elders died. The knowing faded. The agreements did not.

The Seer follows the waking: the last compact-keeper moving between places where ordinary explanations have begun to fail. In each case, a young person notices what everyone else has learned to overlook.
